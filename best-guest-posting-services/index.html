If you want the short answer: the best guest posting services in 2026 are the ones that show you the site before they publish, prove the site has real organic traffic, and replace the link if it disappears. Price matters less than those three things. A $70 link on a genuine niche blog will do more for you than a $400 link on a site that sells posts to anyone who pays.

This guide compares seven providers, from big agencies to self-serve marketplaces to a solo specialist. It covers what each one costs, who it suits, and where it falls short. You'll also find a vetting checklist you can use on any guest post offer, including ones that land in your inbox.

Disclosure: One of the services listed below has a business connection to the author. Prices and policies were taken from each provider's public website in October 2026. They change often, so check before you order.

01What a guest posting service actually does

A guest posting service gets an article published on someone else's website with a link back to yours. Depending on the provider, the work includes some or all of these steps:

  • Finding websites in your niche that accept contributed articles
  • Contacting editors and agreeing on a topic
  • Writing the article
  • Getting it published with your link inside the main content
  • Sending you a report with the live URL

The link is the main reason most people buy. When a relevant, trusted site links to you, Google takes it as a sign that your page is worth ranking. A good placement can also send real visitors, which is often overlooked.

Google's position is worth knowing. Its spam policies say links that are paid for should carry a rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow" attribute. In practice, most guest posting services sell dofollow links anyway, and the risk sits with the buyer. The safest approach is to treat guest posts as one part of your link profile rather than all of it, and to choose sites that publish real editorial content rather than pages built only to sell links.

02How we compared these services

We didn't rank providers by who shouts loudest. Each service was judged on five things you can check yourself:

  1. Site transparency. Can you see or approve the domain before publishing?
  2. Traffic standards. Does the provider verify organic traffic, or only Domain Rating (DR)?
  3. Price clarity. Are prices listed openly, and do they include the article?
  4. Guarantees. What happens if the link is removed later?
  5. Fit. Who is the service actually built for: beginners, agencies, or hands-on SEOs?

04The best guest posting services, reviewed

1FatJoe

FatJoe has been selling blogger outreach for years and is popular with agencies that resell links to their own clients. Pricing is listed openly by DR tier. DR 10+ starts at $72, DR 30+ (their most popular tier) is $120, and DR 60+ is $456. Every placement includes a written article, and they guarantee DR and traffic at the time of placement.

What works: Clear prices, a lifetime link replacement promise, and a white-label dashboard that agencies like.

Watch out for: The lower tiers allow sites with very little traffic (0–1,000 visits a month at DR 10+). If traffic matters to you, start at DR 30+ or higher.

Best for: Agencies and freelancers who need predictable pricing at volume.

2Outreach Monks

Outreach Monks prices by DR tier, from $99 for DR 20+ up to $399 for DR 70+. Their DR 40+ tier at $179 is the most commonly chosen. Each order includes a 750-word article, two rounds of editing, and a live Google Sheet so you can follow progress.

What works: You approve each domain before outreach starts. That one step removes most of the risk of ending up on a site you'd never have picked.

Watch out for: The replacement guarantee is six months, shorter than some competitors. Delivery takes two to three weeks.

Best for: Buyers who want control over where their links appear without doing the outreach themselves.

3Loganix

Loganix is one of the pricier options and makes no apology for it. Their Essential tier starts at $200 per post on DR 30+ sites. The Authority tier starts at $400 on niche-relevant sites with at least 1,000 monthly organic visits. Turnaround is about three weeks.

What works: Strong traffic standards on the Authority tier, plus the option to browse their vetted site database yourself or let their team choose.

Watch out for: At $200 and up, it's hard to justify for a small site that only needs a handful of links to get moving.

Best for: Established businesses competing on difficult keywords, where one strong placement beats five weak ones.

4Rhino Rank

Rhino Rank offers guest posts from $75 and curated links (links added to existing, already-ranking articles) from $60. Guest posts are 750+ words. Delivered links come with 12 months of replacement cover, and first orders have a money-back guarantee up to $550.

What works: Being able to mix fresh guest posts with links in older, indexed content is useful. Curated links often get picked up by Google faster.

Watch out for: The pricing page doesn't break prices down by DR, so you'll need to ask what you're getting at each level.

Best for: SEOs who want variety in their link types from one provider.

5Adsy

Adsy is a self-serve marketplace rather than an agency. You browse a large catalogue of publisher sites, filter by niche, DR, traffic and country, then order directly. Placements range from roughly $15 to over $1,000. Payment sits in escrow until the post goes live, and links are monitored for at least three months.

What works: Huge choice, and you can find bargains if you know how to spot good sites.

Watch out for: Quality across the catalogue varies a lot. Many sites exist mainly to sell posts. You'll need to vet every site yourself (see the checklist below).

Best for: Experienced SEOs who enjoy picking sites and want to keep costs down.

6Collaborator

Collaborator is another marketplace, with a focus on verified traffic. Publishers can connect Google Analytics so buyers see real visitor numbers rather than tool estimates. Most placements fall between about $40 and $90, and links have three months of protection.

What works: Verified analytics are a big step up from relying on third-party traffic estimates.

Watch out for: Some users report high platform commissions, and like any marketplace, the site list still needs your judgment.

Best for: Hands-on buyers who want better data than most marketplaces provide.

7SEO by Subham

SEO by Subham is a solo link-building specialist rather than an agency, run by an SEO who has been in the industry since 2014 and has completed more than 2,000 orders on Fiverr. The service does manual outreach for guest posts, niche edits and foundational links. Pricing is quoted per project after the scope is agreed over email or WhatsApp.

What works: You deal with the person doing the work, not an account manager. Every guest post link is dofollow. If a publisher removes a link later, you get a replacement or a full refund. Monthly clients can follow progress in a shared Google Sheet that's updated daily.

Watch out for: No public price list and no self-serve checkout. Payment is via PayPal after you agree the scope. It doesn't take casino, CBD or adult niches.

Best for: Small businesses and site owners who prefer direct communication with one specialist over a large dashboard.

05Agency, marketplace, freelancer, or DIY?

There's no single "best" model. It depends on your budget, your time, and how much you trust your own judgment on site quality.

Agencies (FatJoe, Outreach Monks, Loganix, Rhino Rank) handle everything and give you guarantees. You pay more per link, but you save hours. Good if your time is worth more than the price difference.

Marketplaces (Adsy, Collaborator) are cheaper and faster, but you do the vetting. Great for experienced buyers, risky for beginners who can't yet tell a real blog from a link farm.

Solo specialists and freelancers sit in between. Prices are usually lower than big agencies, and you get direct contact, but capacity is limited and quality depends entirely on the individual. Check their track record and reviews closely.

DIY outreach costs nothing but time. You find sites, pitch editors and write the articles yourself. The links tend to be the most natural, but expect a low reply rate. Many cold pitches get no answer at all.

One tip from years of watching link budgets: don't spread your money across twenty cheap links. Three or four placements on sites that actually rank and get visitors in your niche will usually move the needle more.

07How to vet a guest post site before you pay

Whatever provider you use, run these checks on any site you're offered. They take about five minutes per site.

  1. Check organic traffic trend. Use Ahrefs, Semrush or a similar tool. You want a stable or rising line. A sharp drop often means Google has penalised the site.
  2. Look at which keywords bring traffic. If the site ranks for terms in your niche, great. If it ranks for random stuff unrelated to its topic, that's a warning sign.
  3. Scan recent posts. Open five of the latest articles. If every one has two or three outbound links to unrelated businesses, the site is selling links to anyone.
  4. Search for "write for us" pages and sponsored tags. A site that openly advertises paid posts in big letters is visible to Google too.
  5. Check indexation. Search site:example.com on Google. If few pages show up compared to how many the site has published, Google may not trust it.
  6. Read an article properly. Would a real person enjoy reading it? If the writing is thin or obviously spun, your link will sit in poor company.
  7. Confirm the link placement. Ask whether your link goes in the body of the article or only in an author bio. Contextual links in the body are worth more.

08Red flags that should stop you buying

Prices that look too good, like DR 70 links for $20

No sample sites or refusal to share the domain before publishing

Guarantees of "first-page rankings" within a set time

Sites where the same author has written about cooking, crypto, and car insurance in one week

A provider that won't put its replacement or refund policy in writing

Pressure to buy large bundles before you've tested a single link

09Frequently asked questions

What are the top 10 guest posting sites?+

There isn't a fixed top-ten list that works for everyone. The best guest posting sites for you are the ones that rank in your own niche and get real readers. A tech startup and a home décor brand need completely different publishers. Use the vetting checklist above to build your own list, or ask a provider to share niche-matched options before you order.

Which guest posting agency is the best in India?+

Several well-known guest posting providers are run from India, and many serve clients in the US, UK and Australia. Instead of picking one by reputation, compare them on the same five points used in this guide: site transparency, traffic standards, clear pricing, written guarantees, and direct communication. A provider that lets you approve sites and puts its refund terms in writing is a safer bet than a cheaper one that doesn't.

How much does guest posting cost?+

Most guest posts with writing included cost between $70 and $250 on decent mid-level sites. Low-authority sites can go for under $50, while placements on DR 70+ sites or well-known publications run from $400 to well over $1,000. Niche, traffic and whether content is included all change the price.

How do I find guest posting opportunities on my own?+

Search Google for your topic plus phrases such as "write for us", "contribute", or "guest post guidelines". Look at where competitors' backlinks come from using a backlink tool. Follow editors in your niche on LinkedIn, since many post when they're looking for contributors. Then pitch a specific, useful topic idea instead of sending a generic request.

What are the best free guest posting sites for 2026?+

Free publishing platforms like Medium, LinkedIn Articles, Dev.to and Hashnode let anyone publish without paying. They're good for building a personal brand and reaching readers, but their links are usually nofollow, so they carry less direct SEO value. Free guest posting on independent blogs still exists, mostly through genuine outreach where you offer a strong article in return for a mention.

Are guest posts still worth it in 2026?+

Yes, when they're placed on relevant sites with real traffic. Guest posts stop working when they're bought in bulk from link farms. Treat each placement as content real people should read, and it will keep its value.

10Final thoughts

Choosing among the best guest posting services comes down to how much control you want and how much you can spend. If you want everything done for you with strong guarantees, an agency like Outreach Monks or Loganix makes sense. If you're experienced and budget-conscious, a marketplace like Collaborator gives you more choice for less. If you'd rather talk to one person who handles your links start to finish, a solo specialist can be a good fit.

Whichever route you pick, run the vetting checklist on every site. A few careful placements on real, relevant websites will always beat a pile of cheap links on sites nobody visits.
